Angelo E. Thalassinidis, Ph.D.

Assistant Dean, School of Science, Technology, and Education, Anne Arundel Community College · Cofounder, Workforce 2100

Angelo E. Thalassinidis studies how artificial intelligence is changing work, and what colleges should teach because of it.

He is Assistant Dean of the School of Science, Technology, and Education at Anne Arundel Community College in Arnold, Maryland, and a cofounder of Workforce 2100. His career spans more than 35 years across management consulting, data analytics and business intelligence.

In New York, he held senior roles with firms such as Computer Horizons, AT&T GIS, The Rusting Group and MCI Systemhouse, where he built enterprise business intelligence and data warehousing practices. He later co-founded i-RED Inc. in Athens and Cyprus and led it as Managing Director and CIO, growing a real estate data-aggregation platform from concept to a multimillion-dollar market leader. The platform combined more than 70 data sources across three countries into housing and economic indices used to assess loan exposure and package loans for the secondary market, along with annual studies of local real estate supply and demand.

His research applies the same approach, many public data sources turned into numbers people can act on, to AI and work. It asks three questions. Which work will AI take on, and where? What does that mean for the occupations and academic programs that prepare people for it? And what does AI fluency mean when people hand work to AI agents? He publishes the methods, data and papers on this site so anyone can check and reuse them, and writes many of the papers with co-authors, including Lance Bowen and Athanasios Nathanail.

Outside the college, he organizes Google Developer Groups Annapolis and serves as a Northeast regional mentor, coordinating more than 60 GDG chapters with about 30,000 members.

He holds a Ph.D. in information management from Stevens Institute of Technology, an M.S. in computer science from New York Institute of Technology, and a B.S. in computer science and mathematics from the University of La Verne.

Workforce 2100 reports, which apply this research for states, counties and colleges, are at workforce2100.ai.

Lance Bowen, Ph.D.

Dean, School of Science, Technology, and Education, Anne Arundel Community College · Cofounder, Workforce 2100

Lance Bowen, Ph.D., is a cofounder of Workforce 2100 and Dean of the School of Science, Technology, and Education at Anne Arundel Community College in Arnold, Maryland, a post he has held since 2017. With more than 26 years of experience in higher education, he oversees programs in the sciences, technology, engineering, architecture and construction, and education, and has secured millions in external grants and contracts for the college.

He has expertise in program accreditation and extensive experience as a peer evaluator for the Middle States Commission on Higher Education, including service as a visiting-team chair, and for the Northwest Commission on Colleges and Universities, and his work includes program assessment and grant evaluation.

Before coming to Maryland he was a biology professor and later dean of sciences at Truckee Meadows Community College in Reno, Nevada. He holds a Ph.D. in environmental toxicology and health from the University of Nevada, Reno.
