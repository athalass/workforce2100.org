If AI changes your job, where can your skills take you?

Pick a state and an occupation. For occupations expected to shrink, we show the three growing occupations that use the most of the same skills, according to our model and today's data, and the training and apprenticeships that lead there.

How to read this

Bridges. For every occupation our model expects to shrink in a state, the three growing occupations whose skills, knowledge and work activities (O*NET) overlap the most, among those within reach of the worker's education and pay. Skills in common is that overlap; bridges with less than 40% in common are not shown.

You already have, skills to add. O*NET skills and knowledge areas that both jobs rate as important, beyond what most jobs need, and the ones the new job rates much higher than the current one (O*NET 31 importance ratings).

Close, Stretch, Retrain. Close: most skills carry over, no extra training, similar or better pay. Stretch: at least half the skills carry over, with up to about a year and a half of training. Retrain: longer training, fewer skills in common, or lower pay.

Training. College programs: IPEDS completions at the level the destination job needs. Short-term programs: the state's Eligible Training Provider List (ETPL), matched by the occupation codes each provider lists for its program; the program's field-of-study code is used only when the provider lists no occupation. Apprenticeships: registered apprenticeable occupations (U.S. Department of Labor and state lists).

Limits. Estimates, not predictions or advice. Robots and other physical automation are not yet included, and we do not yet track agentic AI. Local conditions, and the person, matter more than any model. Explore the data · Methods
