Open research on AI, work and education

How AI is changing work, and how people and colleges can keep up.

Workforce 2100 studies how AI is changing work, from today's jobs to the long horizon of this century. We forecast which occupations, academic programs and places change most as AI spreads. We measure what AI is really worth once human checking and errors are counted. And we build frameworks and instruments to teach and assess AI fluency. Papers, models and data, all in the open.

What we study

Four connected areas of work. A framework explains how AI enters work. We use it to measure the effect on the economy, then on jobs and the academic programs that prepare people for them. The last area turns what we learn into how we teach.

01 · FRAMEWORK

How AI enters work

How can we understand, and predict, where AI takes on work?

A framework for the impact of AI as it moves from generative tools to agents that act on their own and, next, to robots. AI takes on a task when it is cheaper than a person after paying for review and for errors. How much people trust it depends on what an error costs and how complex the work is. The human side of that equation is AI fluency: judging what to delegate and checking the result.

02 · ECONOMY

What AI is worth, and where

How much value does AI really create, and who feels it first?

We apply the framework across the U.S. economy. Only about a fifth of AI's headline value is realizable once checking and errors are counted. Automation could remove about 13.6% of labor demand by 2035 before growth, and the wages at stake are highest in high-wage states. We also show how more fluent reviewers raise the value AI delivers.

03 · JOBS & PROGRAMS

Where work is moving

Which jobs grow, shrink or change, and what does that mean for the programs that train for them?

We trace the impact to 830 occupations, showing how much of each job AI takes on and who should stay in charge of the decisions. Then we map it to 1,432 academic program families at every award level, sorting them into Invest, Reinvent and Review tiers so colleges can plan their portfolio.

04 · EDUCATION

Teaching for AI fluency

How should we teach, assess and design programs for work shared with AI?

An academic framework that matches AI use to the stakes of a task and to what the learner still needs to learn, with an instrument to measure that judgment. We test it with leaders and practitioners, track how colleges commit to it, and turn it into new curriculum, starting with an Applied AI associate degree.

How we work

Labor-market modeling

Task-level models built on federal employment and wage data, O*NET task data and observed AI use, run across many scenarios with ranges, not single numbers.

Document analysis

Coding college mission statements, strategic plans and core competencies across U.S. community colleges to track institutional commitments.

Surveys & instruments

Our framework

AI fluency is a judgment, not a course.

Most AI programs stop at the second rung: knowing what the tools are and how to use them. Work now asks for the third: deciding when to delegate to AI, checking the result and answering for it. That is what we teach toward and what we measure.

RUNG 3
AI Fluency

AI Literacy
