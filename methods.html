Method

How the numbers are built

Every number on this site comes from the same four steps. Below, one occupation, bookkeeping, accounting and auditing clerks (1.37 million jobs in 2025), goes through all four, with the real numbers from our model.

1. Does AI pay for this task?

The unit is a task, not a job. A bookkeeper's job is 30 tasks in O*NET.

Each source answers a different question about the same task:

  • What is the work? O*NET lists each occupation's tasks. Core tasks count twice as much as supplemental ones. O*NET's rating of how important it is to be exact or accurate tells us how costly a mistake is in the job.
  • Could AI do it? Eloundou et al. (2024) label each task: not exposed, exposed, or exposed with extra tools (counted at three quarters).
  • How well does it work in practice? The Anthropic Economic Index (January 2026) shows how often AI succeeds at the task and how much of the original time a person still spends working with it and checking it. Tasks seen in fewer than 30 conversations take the typical values of their occupation, or of their occupation group.
  • Does it pay? AI has to cost less than the person who does the task now, once checking and mistakes are paid for.
For each hour of exposed work:
AI's full cost = AI cost + checking + failures
                = c + wv·v + (1 − p)·[d·wd + (1 − d)·L]
c: AI's cost per hour of work ($2 in our 2035 case). wv·v: checking time at an experienced worker's wage (75th percentile). 1 − p: how often AI fails; 90% of failures (d) are caught and redone at the entry-level wage wd (25th percentile), 10% slip through and cost L = 5 × that wage × how costly mistakes are in the job. AI pays when its full cost is below wd.

Bookkeeping clerks: "operate accounting software to record, store and analyze information"

Entry-level wage (wd)$20.92 an hour
AI succeeds today / in our 2035 case (failures halve)42% / 71%
AI cost per hour$2.00
Checking: $29.55 × 5.5% of the time$1.63
Failures: 29% × (90% redone at $20.92 + 10% missed at $122)$9.06
AI's full cost, below the $20.92 wage: AI pays≈ $12.70

People keep the checking time and the redoing of caught failures, about 32% of each exposed hour. The other 68% of the hour is freed.

Not every task passes. Legal secretaries' tasks fail the test today: AI succeeds only 27% of the time, so redoing and missed errors cost more than the wage.

On the site: AI value per hour in Jobs & programs is this test with today's values ($5 an hour, today's success rates): the wage minus AI's full cost (The Price of Judgment).

2. How much of the job is that?

Add up the tasks, each weighted by how much of the job it is.

Freed share = Σ (weight × exposure × pays × freed) ÷ Σ weight
For each task: its weight (core 2, supplemental 1), its exposure (0, 0.75 or 1), whether AI pays (1 or 0) and the freed share of its hour.

Bookkeeping clerks: all 30 tasks

Work hours AI could do (exposed)90%
Work hours where AI could do the task and it pays90%

For bookkeeping the cost test removes almost nothing: AI is cheap and reliable enough on these tasks. The 23-point gap between 90% and 67% is the checking and redoing that stays with people.

On the site: the share of work hours where AI pays (90% here) is the work hours measure for programs, the AI line and the curriculum change level in the college profiles; the skills view carries it from tasks to work activities.

3. How much of it happens by 2035?

Employers do not all adopt at once, and some work keeps a person in charge.

  • How fast employers adopt AI. The Census Business Trends and Outlook Survey (November 2025 to October 2026) shows the share of firms using AI, weighted by employment. We project it to 2035 with a standard adoption curve and allow firms several years to go from first use to full use. By the end of 2035 that comes to about 85% of the work where AI pays.
  • Who decides. Where mistakes are costly or the work is complex, AI moves more slowly: by 15% (complex work), 25% (costly mistakes) or 40% (both). Bookkeeping is in the "AI assists" group: costly mistakes, routine work.
  • More of the same work. When a job gets cheaper to do, people buy more of it. Between 10% and 50% of freed hours go into doing more, depending on the occupation group; 10% for office support.

4. What happens to people?

Work AI takes over is only half the picture. Jobs are also created.

  • Growth without AI. Each occupation's growth from the BLS 2025–35 projections. BLS already lowered 44 occupations because of AI; those take the growth of similar occupations, so AI is not counted twice.
  • New jobs from the money AI saves. We keep BLS's total for US employment, so the work AI removes is replaced by new demand. The savings are spent the way BLS projects spending to grow, and BLS tables turn that spending into jobs by industry and occupation. These jobs lean toward health care, personal care and food service.
  • Net change = growth without AI + new jobs − work AI takes over. Occupations where it is negative shrink; the people in those lost jobs may need to change occupation.

Bookkeeping clerks, 2025–2035

Added up over every occupation that shrinks, about 9.7 million people (6.2% of jobs) work in occupations with fewer jobs in 2035. Total US jobs still grow, because health care, personal care and other occupations grow more.

Slow recovery. If only 75% of the displaced work is replaced by 2035, every occupation grows less, and more people are in shrinking occupations. The briefs show this as a second number.

On the site: net change and the outlook in Jobs & programs (and, for programs, the demand for graduates and the tier); jobs lost in shrinking occupations in the briefs and on the States/Counties tab; the Career Bridge Finder starts from the occupations that shrink.

States, counties and colleges

States and counties use the same occupation rates, with the cost test rerun at each state's own wages, applied to each place's own mix of jobs (OEWS May 2025 for states, QCEW and ACS for counties). Where people live comes from LODES commuting data. Differences between places come from what kind of work they do; local population and industry trends are not modeled. College programs are linked to the occupations their graduates enter (Census ACS for bachelor's and graduate degrees, the CIP–SOC crosswalk for certificates and associate degrees).

What is not included yet

Robots and other physical automation, and agentic AI that carries out multi-step work on its own beyond what AI does today. Normal job turnover inside growing occupations. These are model estimates, not forecasts of layoffs; the ranges above show how much they can move.
