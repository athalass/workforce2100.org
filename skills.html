Which skills behind a program will AI take on?

Pick a program. For the jobs its graduates go into, we show which skills and work activities AI is likely to take on by 2035, which stay with people, and what that means for the curriculum, according to our model and today's data.

How to read this

Where graduates go. Each program family is linked to the jobs its graduates go into: for bachelor's and graduate awards, the occupations of recent graduates with that field of degree (ACS 2024); for certificates and associate degrees, the NCES CIP-SOC crosswalk weighted by employment. These are the same weights as the program tiers.

Share AI can take on. Every task in those jobs (O*NET 31) is tested: can AI do it, and does it cost less than a person once AI errors, checking time and the stakes are counted? Tasks are linked to the work activities they involve, and O*NET links each skill to work activities. A skill's share is the share of the work using it that AI can take on.

Importance. O*NET importance ratings (1 to 5) for the program's jobs, weighted the same way.

Limits. National figures for the whole program family; a college's own mix of programs and local jobs can differ. Estimates, not predictions or advice. Robots and other physical automation are not yet included, and we do not yet track agentic AI. Explore the data · Methods
